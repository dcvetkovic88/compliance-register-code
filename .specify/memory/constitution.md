## Article I — Product boundaries

1. The suite is **two products, one loop**: RegTech Nexus (gap-analysis engine, "compliance on paper") and Covenant (compliance orchestration and evidence layer, "compliance on the ground"), joined by one shared regulatory knowledge graph. Nexus decides what must be proven; Covenant proves it; the evidence chain never breaks in between.
2. **Each product must remain independently sellable and deployable** (commercial rule, Jul 24 and Aug 21). Nexus stands alone as the entry product. Covenant never ships without the Nexus knowledge layer: in a Covenant-led deal, Nexus is packaged inside as the regulatory content service. No integration feature may create a runtime hard dependency that breaks either product when the other is absent (degradation rules in the integration spec).
3. Nexus is a **gap-analysis engine**, not a per-regulation application. Core loop: authoritative regulation in, client documentation in, gap report out. DORA is the first authoritative source loaded into the engine; it is data, not architecture.
4. Every assessed clause receives exactly one of four verdicts: **Covered**, **Missing**, **Additional**, **Not needed**. Every verdict carries a semantic explanation a compliance officer would trust, grounded in the specific regulation text and the specific client clause.
5. Regulation as configuration: regulations are versioned inputs, never code. Adding NIS2 or CSRD means loading a new authoritative source into the same pipeline, zero rebuild.
6. Covenant is an **orchestration and evidence layer**, not a scanner, SIEM, or GRC re-implementation. It maps regulation-first: obligation → required evidence type → source system type → connector. Security platforms (WiseBee, CheckPoint, and similar) plug in under the Covenant umbrella as **evidence and data feeds only**; the compliance layer itself is always Covenant/Nexus territory (framing rule, Jul 28, unchanged). Clarification (v0.4.3, 2026-10-17): an ICT risk register limited to the regulated ICT risk management obligations (DORA Pillar 1b, national ICT standards such as NBS Decision RS OG 102/2024), whose residual risk is derived from Covenant control status and whose records exist to produce obligation-anchored evidence, is part of the evidence layer (feature 107). Enterprise GRC stays out: risk types beyond ICT, policy lifecycle management, internal audit management, generic issue and workflow suites.
7. Integration boundary: the ONLY coupling between the two products is the shared grain (obligation id + regulation version) and the versioned contracts C1-C5 (contracts/). Neither product reaches into the other's internals, schemas, or storage.

## Article II — Technology boundaries

1. Storage: **one PostgreSQL engine per client deployment** serves vector (pgvector), graph, relational workflow state, and append-only audit. No second database. When Nexus and Covenant are co-deployed, they share this one engine and the suite foundation (queue, audit, auth); a standalone Covenant deployment stands up the same foundation stack. Justification is operational simplicity and single-transaction consistency, not raw traversal speed.
2. Retrieval framework: **LightRAG (HKUDS) on the Postgres backend**, hard version pinning, schema ownership, wrapped behind our own API. Upgrades are risk events; the team owns the upgrade path and budgets for a possible fork.
3. Graph traversal (Regulation → Obligation → Control → Evidence): **Apache AGE by default**, fixed-depth typed queries only; bulk ingestion outside Cypher MERGE. Fallback to plain recursive CTEs per the Phase 0 trial. The graph layer stays behind an internal traversal API as a standing hedge; SQL/PGQ is NOT committed to any released PostgreSQL as of Sep 2026 (validated 2026-09-15), so no migration date is assumed and the CTE fallback is the only near-term alternative. Exactly one traversal mechanism in production at a time.
4. Embeddings: a **multi-model ensemble, open pool, no fixed count**. A model earns and keeps its seat only by adding unique catches on the ground-truth eval set that justify its cost. Ensemble agreement drives confidence bucketing. Every ensemble member must be commercially licensed for self-hosted on-prem use (sovereignty constraint).
5. Explanatory layer: **grounded prompting with strict guardrails, no fine-tuning at start, model-independent by design**. Retrieve the evidence, force citation, constrain output format, reject any explanation not referencing actual source spans. Fine-tuning only after real compliance-officer corrections form a training set (Phase 2+).
6. Language and stack: Python backend; React + Tailwind frontend (white-label capable, one shared shell library across both products). Covenant connectors follow the connector framework (COV-S5): connectors are written per system TYPE, normalize to the evidence-record grain, and **MCP is the preferred protocol for partner platform feeds** (WiseBee confirmed Jul 28).
7. **Hybrid topology (amends v0.2.0 II.6, per the Aug 21 decision)**: the suite has two planes.
   - The **authoritative plane** (regulation sources, extraction, the unified regulation knowledge base) may run as a Wiser-operated cloud service serving all client deployments; it holds regulation content only, never client data.
   - The **client plane** (client documents, verdicts, controls, evidence, connectors, audit) never leaves the client deployment boundary. No client data ever flows to the cloud plane, in any tier.
   - **Full on-prem remains a supported tier**: the regulation KB is then delivered as versioned, signed content bundles by file transfer; the human approval gate applies to bundle import exactly as to live sync. Apart from the KB sync channel and the DMZ-isolatable crawler, every client-plane service runs without outbound internet. No cloud-only managed dependencies inside the client plane.

## Article III — Security, compliance, and oversight

1. Data sovereignty: client documents, evidence, and derived data never leave the client deployment boundary. No training on client data unless contractually agreed per client. Evidence payloads are never used to train or tune any model.
2. **EU AI Act compliance by design**: the suite likely sits in the high-risk category; meaningful human oversight, transparency, and record-keeping are architectural requirements from day one.
3. **Human approval gate**: no auto-ingested regulatory change goes live in client-facing assessments without expert sign-off, whether delivered via cloud KB sync or via air-gapped content bundle. Automate detection and heavy lifting; gate the go-live.
4. **Version-stamp everything**: every obligation, every gap verdict, every control status, and every evidence record states which regulation version it was judged against. Regulatory diffs are computed at the obligation level; already-assessed clients re-check only the delta, and Covenant re-proves only the affected controls.
5. Audit trail is append-only and immutable (insert-only tables, revoked UPDATE/DELETE); retention configurable, minimum 5 years. RBAC on every surface; access decisions logged. In a co-deployed suite there is ONE audit trail spanning both products, correlated end to end (a regulatory change is traceable from crawl to re-proof through one correlation id).
6. Verdicts without a complete explainability record are rejected at persistence. Evidence records without source, hash, timestamp, and obligation anchor are rejected at persistence (the Covenant mirror of this rule).
7. Incident reporting artifacts (Covenant Pillar 2) carry the regulator deadline chain as machine-readable deadlines, with the anchors the reporting RTS actually defines (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301, Art. 5, EUR-Lex eli/reg_del/2025/301/oj; validated 2026-09-15, primary-source pin added after Quorum panel-20260915-002): initial notification within 4h of classification as major AND no later than 24h from awareness (earlier bound wins); intermediate report within 72h of SUBMITTING the initial notification; final report within one month of submitting the intermediate report or its latest update; next-working-day-noon extension where the RTS allows it, with entity-type carve-outs as tenant configuration. Windows and anchors are catalog data (C1), never hard-coded. The platform must make the deadline state visible at all times.

## Article V — Spec discipline

1. Spec-completeness gate: a spec is complete when the executor (Claude Code) can implement it without asking a design-changing question. Acceptance criteria must be testable statements.
2. Abandon-and-respec: when implementation reveals a design-changing spec error, stop, update the spec, re-review, resume.
3. Hierarchy: constitution (L0) → platform specs (L1: docs/suite-spec.md above docs/platform-spec.md, docs/covenant-spec.md, docs/integration-spec.md) → service specs (L2: docs/services/ for Nexus, docs/services/covenant/ for Covenant) → feature specs (L3, spec/plan/tasks, just-in-time). Lower levels may not contradict higher levels.
4. Feature numbering ranges: **000-099 Nexus, 100-199 Covenant, 200-299 integration, 300-399 KB service (authoritative plane)**. A feature lives in exactly one range; cross-range work is split. The 300 range exists because the KB service is neither Nexus client-plane code nor integration glue: it is the Wiser-operated authoritative plane (or its bundle-builder equivalent for the full on-prem tier) and follows its own deployment cadence.
5. Synthetic eval fixtures follow the strict separation rule: generator models must differ from ensemble and explanatory models. No model grades its own homework.
6. **Never guess (added v0.5.0).** An unknown API shape, field name, enum value, error code, value domain or external-system behaviour is written into the spec or plan as `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: <question>]` and is never filled with a plausible assumption. There is no cap on the number of markers. This rule sits beside the existing `[RECONSTRUCTED]` and `[DERIVED]` markers: those label provenance, this one labels an open question. A feature or slice with an open marker in its scope does not pass the planning-done gate (VIII.2).

## Article VI — Definition of Done

1. MVP DoD (per feature, both products): acceptance criteria pass via automated tests; explainability, version-stamping, and audit requirements met; deployable via the standard install; documented API surface. Added v0.5.0, for every block that does not complete under v0.4.3 rules (VIII.9): every HTTP operation the block adds or changes is in `contracts/api/` and has a passing conformance check (VIII.5); the engineering standards in `docs/standards/` are followed, or a deviation is recorded in the feature's plan.md Complexity Tracking and accepted by Dusan when that plan lands (the amendment procedure); each service package the block touches has a current ARCHITECTURE.md (VIII.4); for a feature or slice under the planning-done gate, `/speckit-analyze` on it reports no CRITICAL or HIGH finding.
2. Production DoD (adds): security review; observability; error budget; data retention and timed backup/restore verified; upgrade path tested (including pinned LightRAG/AGE versions).
3. The demo target is the first vertical slice per product plus the closed-loop suite demo (feature 201); MVP features may not ship demo-only to the MVP branch.

3. Phasing: Nexus keeps Phase 0-5 (0 Spec Foundation → 1 Gap-Analysis Spine → 2 Explanation & Confidence → 3 Regulation-as-Configuration Proof → 4 Pilot Hardening → 5 Scale). L3 features declare their phase. Reconciliation with the source spec's 17 milestones remains pending its arrival; the 200-page consolidated spec (D13 condition) is due at M1 regardless.
4. Marketing honesty rule (carried from the Jul 21/Aug 21 decisions): capabilities that exist only as deck claims (named partner connectors, aspirational models) are marked as such in the specs and must not be presented as built. 5. **Early slices (added v0.4.2, 2026-10-16).** A feature may be built ahead of its declared phase only as a DECLARED SLICE: declared in the feature spec and approved by Dusan, the approval recorded in that spec's status line; and only when all four hold: (a) the slice depends on merged work alone, and no unmerged feature is stubbed or simulated in its runtime path (test doubles live in tests only); (b) the feature spec names every deferred requirement and acceptance criterion, the slice that will carry it, and the dependency that defers it, including dependencies not yet specified; (c) the slice meets the MVP Definition of Done (VI.1) for the requirements it claims and goes to the MVP branch (main) as production code, never demo-only (VI.3); (d) the phase and the MVP still require the complete feature, so a slice never counts as its phase delivered. Pulling a slice forward changes delivery order only: it moves no gate, no contract and no MVP boundary, and the milestone table in docs/delivery/project-plan.md records it. ## Article VIII: Spec Kit execution (added v0.5.0, 2026-10-29)

This article says how the `/speckit-*` commands are run on this program and what they must produce. It changes no product scope.

1. **Feature artifact set.** Planning happens in the spec repo (the editing home), with the code repo mounted read-only for reference. A feature directory `specs/NNN-slug/` holds: `spec.md` (WHAT and WHY), `plan.md` (HOW), `research.md` (decisions, each with rationale, alternatives rejected, owner and date), `data-model.md` (every table, column, type, nullability, key, constraint, index, state machine and migration it implies), `quickstart.md` (how to exercise the feature end to end on the dev stack), `contracts/` (an OpenAPI 3.1 draft per service whose HTTP API the feature adds or changes; omitted only when there is none, said so in plan.md), `checklists/requirements.md` (spec quality checklist) and `tasks.md`. The shapes are the templates in `.specify/templates/`. For a feature delivered in declared slices (VII.5) the set may be completed per slice, provided every artifact states which slice each part covers.
2. **Planning-done gate.** The first code block of a feature or declared slice starts only when (a) its artifact set is complete for that scope, (b) its contract drafts are promoted into `contracts/api/` (VIII.5), (c) `/speckit-analyze` reports no CRITICAL or HIGH finding, (d) no `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` marker remains in its scope (V.6), and (e) all of it is merged to spec-repo main through the amendment procedure (code repo `docs/SUPERVISOR.md` section 5) and synced into the code repo. For a LATER block of a feature or slice under the gate, (f): if its scope changed after planning-done (a respec under V.2, a new `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]`, an amended contract), the amended artifacts land and sync before that block starts. A builder that finds the gate unmet stops and reports; it does not plan inside a code PR. The first code block means the first block of the feature, or of the declared slice when the feature is delivered in slices. The gate applies to every feature or slice that is not in flight (VIII.9).
3. **Task form.** Every task in `tasks.md` reads `- [ ] T012 [P] [S1] [path] description (FRn, ACn)`: one id; `[P]` only when it touches no file another open task touches; the slice tag when the feature has slices; exactly ONE path it writes: in the code repo a service package, the directory of a grandfathered flat module listed in `docs/standards/conventions.md` section 3, `platform/<module>`, `shell-lib`, `deploy`, `tools`, or `code-repo-root` for the code repo's root documents (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, README.md); or `spec-repo` for a task that changes the spec repo; the requirements and acceptance criteria it serves. A task that writes two paths is a modelling error and is split. Tasks are grouped under `### Block N` headings of two to four tasks, each block one PR, in build order; a block names the check suite that proves it.
4. **Mandatory tasks.** `/speckit-tasks` output is incomplete, and is regenerated, unless it contains: (a) test tasks ordered before or with the code they prove, at the tier the engineering standards require (offline unit tests for pure logic, acceptance suites for acceptance criteria and database guards); (b) one contract conformance task per HTTP operation added or changed, tagged to the package that serves it; (c) one ARCHITECTURE.md task per service package the feature creates or changes, ordered after the code it describes; (d) whenever the feature changes a stack version, the layout, a table prefix, a naming rule or anything else a governing file states (VIII.7), one governing-files task per repository whose governing files change: `[spec-repo]` for the standards and delivery documents, `[code-repo-root]` for the code repo's CLAUDE.md and README.
5. **REST contract-first.** Every HTTP API a suite service exposes (consoles, api-core, admin and ingestion endpoints, the KB gateway) is described by OpenAPI 3.1 at `contracts/api/<service>.openapi.yaml`, under the rules of `docs/standards/api-standards.md`. A feature drafts the operations it adds or changes in `specs/NNN-slug/contracts/` during planning; at planning-done the drafts are merged into the root file operation by operation, never overwriting it, and from then on the root file is the authority and is frozen for implementation: changing it is a spec amendment. Conformance is proved by test against the real endpoint, never against a copy of the model. The C1-C5 JSON Schemas and their rules are unchanged by this clause.
6. **Engineering standards are binding.** `docs/standards/backend-rules.md`, `docs/standards/frontend-rules.md`, `docs/standards/api-standards.md` and `docs/standards/conventions.md` bind all code. Precedence, highest first: this constitution; the feature's spec.md, plan.md, tasks.md and frozen contracts; the engineering standards; a service package's ARCHITECTURE.md; `/speckit-*` skill defaults; the surrounding code. Where the order does not settle a conflict, the agent stops and reports. The standards change only by the amendment procedure. A feature-specific deviation is allowed only as recorded under VI.1 (plan.md Complexity Tracking, accepted by Dusan when the plan lands); it binds that feature only and changes no standard.
7. **Governing files stay true.** `CLAUDE.md` in both repositories, the engineering standards, `docs/delivery/dev-plan.md` and the code repo README describe the system as it is. A change that makes any of them false carries the task that corrects it (VIII.4(d)), in the same feature.
8. **Overrides of Spec Kit skill defaults.** Where a `/speckit-*` skill and this constitution differ, this constitution wins. In particular: `/speckit-specify` does not make "informed guesses" and has no cap on clarification markers (V.6); `/speckit-implement` does not scaffold project structure, does not create or edit ignore files, dependency manifests or CI configuration unless a task names them, and touches only the files its task names; a task that needs more stops and reports.
9. **Transition for work in flight (v0.5.0).** In-flight status is held per feature, or per declared slice when the feature is delivered in slices; a later slice is never in flight by virtue of an earlier one (VII.5). A feature or slice is IN FLIGHT when, at the commit that lands v0.5.0 on spec-repo main, its first code block is merged or a pull request for its first code block is open. A pull request that qualified it and is later closed without merging ends its in-flight status. The planning-done gate does not apply to work in flight, and every remaining block of it completes under the rules of v0.4.3. All other v0.5.0 rules (V.6, VIII.3 to VIII.8) apply to new tasks from that commit on. Every HTTP operation shipped under v0.4.3 rules is listed in `contracts/README.md` under "Conformance owed" and gains its contract entry and conformance check in the first feature that changes it. In-flight status never exempts a pull request from conforming to the CURRENT spec of its feature: a pull request built against a superseded spec is reworked or rebuilt, as Dusan decides. Ruled by Dusan on 2026-11-02 (owner decision 7, option A on all four sub-questions); the features and slices in flight at the landing commit, and the pull request that qualified each, are recorded in `docs/amendments-2026-10-29-spec-standards.md`.

## Article IX: Standalone builds (added v0.6.0, 2026-11-04)

This article governs a STANDALONE BUILD: a separately deployable product that delivers a subset of suite features for one use case and integrates into the suite later. First use: the outsourcing and ICT risk register for Raiffeisen Bank Serbia (docs/standalone/register-standalone-spec.md). Articles I to VIII keep governing the suite code repository unchanged.

1. **Same specification, own code repository.** A standalone build is specified in this repository under the same rules (V, VIII.1 to VIII.4, VIII.6 to VIII.8) and built in its own code repository, named by Dusan at its creation. Its L1 is a document under `docs/standalone/`. It is not the suite MVP branch: VI.3 and VII.5 do not apply to it, and nothing it ships counts as a suite feature, slice or phase delivered. Its Definition of Done is VI.1 without the suite-specific clauses (explainability record for Nexus verdicts, KB version stamps from the live KB): acceptance criteria pass via automated tests, audit and access rules met, deployable by its own documented install, documented API surface.
2. **Polyglot monorepo, coarse services, contract-first.** One repository holds every service of the build. Services are coarse, one per bounded context, each owning its own database schema and migrations and never reading another's tables, except the shared access-control functions and role tables of the platform context that row-level policies call, named in the build's L1. Every synchronous API is described in OpenAPI 3.1 and every event in AsyncAPI 3.0 before code is written; drafts live under `docs/standalone/contracts/` and are frozen at planning-done (VIII.2 applies with this path in place of `contracts/api/`). Conformance is proved against the real endpoint or the real emitted event.
3. **Language rule.** Python is used only for AI and document processing services. Core domain services are written so that a Java or .NET team can take them over: domain logic behind the published contracts, no language-specific coupling across service boundaries, no shared in-process libraries carrying domain rules, persistence behind a repository layer. The user interface stack is chosen per build in its L1. For standalone builds this rule replaces II.6; II.1 (one PostgreSQL engine per deployment) and III.5 (append-only audit, RBAC, access decisions logged) still apply.
4. **Stubs behind ports.** Where a standalone build needs a suite capability not yet available to it (catalog sync, control status, evidence store, audit, identity, queue, connectors, findings), it uses a stand-in behind a port named after the suite element, listed in its L1. Stand-ins are never presented to a client as the built suite (VII.4). Integration replaces adapters, not domain code.
5. **AI data handling (an explicit, bounded exception to III.1 and II.7 for standalone builds).** Every model call goes through one gateway interface so the model provider can be changed by configuration and adapter only. Demonstrations use fictional documents only. For a client's real documents the default is an on-premises model inside the deployment boundary, under which III.1 and II.7 hold as written. Only where the client approves it in writing per deployment may the gateway send document content to an EU-region hosted model endpoint under contractual terms of no training on and no retention of client data; that endpoint is then the single permitted outbound path for client data besides the configured connectors, it carries only the text a functionality's request needs, and the approval, endpoint and region are recorded in the deployment configuration and the audit trail. Without that written approval the exception does not exist and III.1 applies unchanged. AI output is a suggestion: a named human confirms every value before it enters a register, assessment or report (III.2), and the suggestion, its sources and the confirming person are kept.
6. **Feature sub-structure.** A feature carrying standalone functionality keeps its suite `spec.md` and adds a parent `overview.md` and a `functionalities/fN-slug/` folder per functionality, each with `spec.md`, `plan.md` and `tasks.md`, and one `checklists/requirements.md` per feature covering its functionalities. Requirement and acceptance ids inside a functionality are prefixed with the feature and functionality (`104-F2-FR3`, `104-F2-AC3`), never reusing the parent spec's numbers. Research, data model, quickstart and contract drafts shared by the build live under `docs/standalone/`. An acceptance criterion of a parent suite spec is never changed through a functionality (V.2 and the class C rule apply).
7. **Integration.** Each standalone L1 states its integration path: data mapping onto the suite record levels, the port-by-port replacement, and the suite features the build is upsold into. Roles a standalone build adds are mapped onto the suite role model in its access-governance feature before integration.
8. **What binds a standalone build.** Bind: I.5 to I.7, II.1 and II.7 (subject to IX.5), III (subject to IX.5), the evidence freshness gate and the planning-done gate of IV, V, VI.1 as restated in IX.1, VII.1, VII.2, VII.4, and VIII as listed in IX.1. Do not bind: I.1 to I.4 (suite and Nexus product rules, unless the build includes Nexus features), II.2 to II.6 (replaced by IX.3 and the build's L1), the IV gates other than those named, VI.2 and VI.3, VII.3 and VII.5 (suite phasing, MVP and early slices). Where a standalone build's L1 and this list disagree, this list wins. Clarification (v0.6.1, 2026-11-06): In a standalone build, a [NEEDS CLARIFICATION] marker triaged BANK or PILOT does not block the planning-done gate of a functionality's DEMO scope, provided the spec states, next to the marker, the default the demo uses and the requirement is tagged so that the open part is outside [DEMO] scope. Markers triaged DEMO-BLOCKER or PRE-DEMO SPIKE always block planning-done and are resolved before it.
